As is evident in the ECG for \textit{a02} in \ref{ECGs}, not all of the QRS complexes were annotated correctly by the provided annotation file. The difficulty may have arisen from the p-waves almost becoming as tall as the R-peak. 
Since this study's implementation relies on the QRS complexes being annotated accurately, there will be regions where the respiratory rate will appear slower than is actually the case.

The spectrograms for EDRs and chest measurements can be seen in figure \ref{EDRCompare}. For record a01, both spectrograms have 'bright spots' for frequencies between 15-25 beats/min, suggesting that the EDR was able to capture the overall respiratory rate well. On the other hand, for a02, the pattern on the spectrogram is more uniform. Furthermore, In the region $t\in\left[129,176\right]$, most of the frequencies between 4-30 are absent, whereas in the chest measurements, there is a bright band at around 20 beats/min. Nonetheless, there seems to be evidence to suggest that when a person exits a state of apnoea and return to normal breathing, we sometimes observe decreases in intensities of many of the frequencies in the 4-30 breaths/min range.
